Quick add

Write it the way you'd say it.

No pickers, no forms. Quickspan reads dates, times, repeats and places as you type, in English or Spanish, right on your phone.

    Anything from Dentist Thursday 4pm to Gym every Mon and Thu at 7pm.

  2. See what it understood

    The words it recognised light up, and a line shows exactly what will be saved.

  3. Press Enter

    It's on your calendar, with undo. No time given? It finds your first free hour.

Every screen

A calendar that fits the screen you're holding.

Most calendars stretch a phone layout. Quickspan changes its layout with the space it has.

Tablets get the whole week

Seven days with a Today panel beside them, and the event's details next to the grid when you pick one. Drag the divider to resize the panes.

Phones get three days

A readable week on a narrow screen, with the quick-add bar under your thumb.

Foldables, done properly

Half open like a book, the week sits on one side of the hinge and today on the other. Flat on a table, the grid goes above the fold and the controls below. Fold or unfold and you keep your place.

Book posture on a Pixel 10 Pro Fold (emulator)

Keyboard, mouse and dark mode

Shortcuts for everything, right-click menus, Ctrl+scroll to zoom the hours, and a second window beside the first. Events drag between them.

And the rest

Small things that add up.

Your phone's calendars

Show your Google, Exchange and other calendars beside Quickspan's own, and add, edit, move or delete their events. Your phone's account syncs them as always.

Drag and drop

Move an event to another day or time, drag its edge to change its length, or sweep an empty spot to make a new one. Several at once, too.

Undo and redo

Moved or deleted something by mistake? Undo it from the bar that appears, or with Ctrl Z.

Repeating events

Daily, weekdays, weekly, monthly or yearly, with an end date. Change just one occurrence when plans move.

Print or save as PDF

Print the week, or save it as a PDF to share, in landscape with every event in its calendar's colour.

Accessible

TalkBack reads each event as one sentence, every menu action works without a mouse, and large text is supported.

Private by design

Nothing leaves your phone. It can't.

Quickspan doesn't have permission to use the internet. That isn't a promise in a policy: Android enforces it, and you can see it in the permission list on the Play page.

0internet access

The app has no internet permission, so it can't send your events anywhere.

0accounts

Nothing to sign up for. Open it and start typing.

0ads or trackers

The app has no ads, no analytics and no advertising ID. Play's Data safety says "No data collected".

If you choose to show a Google or Exchange calendar, that calendar keeps syncing through your phone's own account, as it always did. Quickspan adds no network access of its own. Read the privacy policy

Questions

Good to know.

Is it really free?

Yes. No ads, no subscription, no in-app purchases. Everything it does today stays free.

Does it sync between my devices?

Not by itself, because it has no internet access. If you want the same events on several devices, keep them in a Google or Exchange calendar on your phone and turn on Phone calendars in Quickspan: your account syncs them.

Which languages does quick add understand?

English and Spanish. The rest of the app is in English and Spanish too, and dates and times follow your phone's settings.

Why does it ask for calendar access?

Only to show and edit the calendars already on your phone, and only when you turn that on. Without it, Quickspan works with its own calendars.

Which phones and tablets does it run on?

Android 8 and newer: phones, tablets, foldables and flip phones, including desktop-style windows on tablets.

What doesn't it do yet?

No time zones (times are your phone's local time), and quick add only speaks English and Spanish. Tell us what you miss at support@quickspan.app.
